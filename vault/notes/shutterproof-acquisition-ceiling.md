## Why the retention-first rule says keep

The whole point of the acquisition and retention thresholds is that retention is expensive
to fix and acquisition is buyable. An app with good retention and bad acquisition is a
spending decision. An app with bad retention and good acquisition is a rebuild.

Shutterproof is the first kind. Twelve weeks of steady install growth from 640 to 992 with
no paid spend at all suggests the ceiling is organic reach, not demand.

## What I am actually worried about

CPI. If paid installs cost more than the LTV supports, buying the last eight installs a week
is a way to lose money slowly while the dashboard turns green. EXP-005 caps this
explicitly: kill the campaign if CPI passes $2.20 after 500 installs, or if the paid cohort
lands under 45% D1.

The second condition matters more than the first. Paid cohorts that retain worse than
organic are a signal that the keywords are attracting the wrong people, and no amount of
bid tuning fixes wrong-fit traffic.

## The cheaper option first

Product Hunt (EXP-007) costs nothing but a day. It is ranked lower on ICE because
confidence is low — we have never launched there and the audience fit is a guess. But if it
works it moves 800 installs in a week without touching CPI at all.

Honestly this ordering might be wrong. Low confidence is not the same as low expected
value when the cost is a day of work.
